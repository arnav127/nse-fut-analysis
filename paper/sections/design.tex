\section{Research design}\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Three groups of securities}

The comparison of interest is between the settlement window on an expiry day and the same
window on other days. Two confounds stand between that comparison and a conclusion about
the settlement mechanism: the market environment of a given month, and the calendar. A
monthly expiry is also a Thursday on which the index options expire, and it falls in the last
days of the month, when index rebalancing and month-end portfolio flows occur. The design
addresses the second confound with three groups of securities and two calendar controls.

The \emph{liquid} and \emph{illiquid} groups are the \SelectGroupSize{} most traded and
\SelectGroupSize{} least traded securities with stock derivatives. Both face the same
settlement incentive; they differ in the cost of acting on it. The \emph{placebo} group
consists of \SelectGroupSize{} securities without derivatives, matched to the illiquid group.
No contract settles on their closing price, but they are subject to everything else that
happens on the same dates. An effect that appears in the placebo group as well belongs to the
calendar.

Membership is derived from the trade files rather than taken from an index list, which would
describe the index at the time of download rather than in the sample year. Derivatives
eligibility is read from the derivatives trade files: a security is treated as having
derivatives when its stock future traded on each of the \ConvSessions{} sessions for which
these files are available, and as having none when neither a future nor an option on it
traded on any of them. Securities that were added to or removed from the derivatives segment
within the period are excluded from both.

Turnover is measured on the \SelectControlSessions{} control sessions only, so that group
membership does not depend on behaviour on expiry days. A security must trade in every
session, clear a floor of \SelectMinTrades{} trades in the median control session, and hold a
stable turnover rank: its rank may not move by more than \SelectMaxRankSpan{}\% of the
cross-section between the tenth and ninetieth percentiles of the year. Placebo securities are
selected by nearest-neighbour matching without replacement on log turnover and log price, so
that differences in tick size relative to price do not produce differences in quoted spreads.
Table~\ref{tab:balance} shows the result. The placebo group matches the illiquid group
closely, and it does not overlap the liquid group: a security as heavily traded as the liquid
group but without derivatives does not exist in this market, so the placebo comparison speaks
to the illiquid group.

\subsection{Three samples}

The analysis uses three samples, each chosen for what its measures require.

\paragraph{Full-year trade sample.} The settlement price, the prices before and after the
window, and the net order flow of each participant category require only the trade file.
These are measured for all \TapeSessions{} trading sessions of 2022, giving
\TapeObservations{} security-sessions. Every session is classified into one of four types:
the \TapeNMonthlyexpiry{} monthly expiries; the \TapeNWeeklyexpiry{} weekly index expiries,
on which the index options settle but no stock derivative does; the \TapeNMonthend{} month
ends that are not expiries; and all other sessions. The weekly expiry and the month end are
the calendar controls. Each shares one feature of the monthly expiry, and neither carries the
stock derivatives settlement.

\paragraph{Order book sample.} Spreads, depth, cancellations and concealed size require the
full order file and a reconstruction of the book. These are measured on the
\DesignExpirySessions{} monthly expiries and a control session in the same month, the
trading day before the expiry, giving \DesignSessions{} sessions.

\paragraph{Derivatives sample.} The derivatives trade files are available for the four
monthly expiries from March to June and their control sessions. They provide the prices of
the expiring futures and the strikes and volumes of the expiring stock options.
