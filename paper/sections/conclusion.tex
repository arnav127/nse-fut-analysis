\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The settlement price of derivatives on the National Stock Exchange of India is the average
price of the last half hour of trading, and it is often argued that this arrangement invites
participants with expiring positions to move the price in that window. This paper tests the
argument with order-level data for every session of 2022, against calendar controls, a placebo
group of securities without derivatives and a placebo index.

The four hypotheses receive different answers. Expiry concentrates trading in the window (H1):
on monthly expiries a larger share of the day's volume is traded in the settlement window, in
securities with derivatives and not in the placebo group, and spreads widen. Order flow in the
window is not dominated by one side on expiry days, but the imbalance that exists persists more
through the window (H2), as the execution of large orders would produce. Whether the
settlement price is displaced (H3) depends on what settles. The stock settlement price is
partly reversed the next morning on monthly expiries, but equally in securities on which
nothing settles, so the reversal is a property of the expiry session. The index settlement is
displaced: on index expiries the Nifty 50 falls over the window relative to the Nifty Next 50,
by about \IdxSpreadDriftPoints{} index points, within the window, in the index's constituents
and not in other stocks.

The settlement method explains the difference (H4). Stock derivatives were settled by
delivery, which makes the settlement price an accounting price for futures holders; the
expiring stock future is accordingly priced on the cash price until the end of the window, and
neither stock nor index settlement prices are drawn toward option strikes. Index derivatives
were settled in cash, which makes the settlement value the payoff itself. The settlement
window is displaced where its price is paid out in cash, and not where it only divides a
delivery payment.

The analysis has limitations. The derivatives files cover four of the twelve monthly expiries,
so the results for stock futures and options rest on a smaller sample. Open interest and
positions are not observed, so the incentive cannot be measured contract by contract, and the
direction of the index displacement cannot be tied to the positions of those who trade in the
window. The categories in the order file distinguish institutional, proprietary and other
clients but not individual participants. The closing value of the index is approximated by its
average over the window. The sample covers a single year, with Thursday expiries. Finally,
evidence of a systematic displacement in the average is not evidence of manipulation by any
particular trader, which requires surveillance data that identifies the trader; a displacement
of the same sign could also arise from the unwinding of hedges at the settlement price.
