\begin{abstract}
\noindent Derivatives on the National Stock Exchange of India settle against the volume-weighted
average price of the last thirty minutes of trading. Using order-level data for 2022, we test
whether the settlement window behaves differently on expiry days under four broad hypotheses,
each with one primary measure: the settlement price, the cost of trading, the one-sidedness of
order flow, and order behaviour and participation. Expiry sessions are compared with control
sessions in the same month, for securities with derivatives and for a matched placebo group
without them. Two primary tests are rejected: on expiry days the quoted spread widens and more
orders are withdrawn within a second of entry, both specifically in securities with
derivatives. Both follow the higher volatility of the window on expiry days: liquidity
suppliers quote wider and thinner and refresh their quotes faster when prices move more. The
settlement VWAP does not move further from the price at the start of the window, and order
imbalance is not more one-sided, in the cash market or in the expiring stock futures, most of
whose positions are rolled before the expiry day. The window is crowded rather than pushed, and
the partial reversal of the settlement price the next morning is accounted for by information
arriving after the session.
Stock futures, settled by delivery, are priced on the cash price until the close, leaving no
gain from moving the settlement. Where settlement is in cash, the index settlement is displaced:
on index expiries the Nifty 50 falls over the window relative to the Nifty Next 50, which has no
derivatives (randomization $p = \IdxSpreadDriftExpiryRip{}$).

\noindent \textbf{Keywords:} expiration-day effects; settlement VWAP; order imbalance; limit order
book; market microstructure. \\
\noindent \textbf{JEL classification:} G13, G14, G18.
\end{abstract}
