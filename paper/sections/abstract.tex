\begin{abstract}
\noindent Stock derivatives on the National Stock Exchange of India settle at the
volume-weighted average price of the last thirty minutes of the expiry session. We ask whether
this window is used to move the settlement price. Using order-level data for \TapeSessions{}
sessions of 2022 and \TapeSecurities{} securities, we compare the window on monthly expiries
with the window on every other session, using weekly index expiries and month ends as
calendar controls and securities without derivatives as a placebo group. On monthly expiries
trading shifts into the window, only in securities with derivatives. The settlement price
moves further over the window and is partly reversed the next morning, but the reversal is
equally large in the placebo group, and no participant category's flow has a larger or less
permanent price effect on expiry days. The expiring stock future, which is settled by
delivery, is priced on the cash price until the end of the window rather than on the
settlement price, and the settlement price shows no attraction to option strikes. Expiry makes
the window busier and more expensive to trade in, but we find no evidence that the
settlement price is displaced by trading aimed at it.

\vspace{0.6em}
\noindent \textbf{Keywords:} expiration-day effects; settlement price; closing price
manipulation; physical settlement; limit order book. \\
\noindent \textbf{JEL classification:} G13, G14, G18.
\end{abstract}
