\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Stock futures on the National Stock Exchange of India settle at the volume-weighted average
price of the underlying cash market over the last half hour of the expiry session. Anyone
holding a large expiring position therefore has a financial interest in a known, narrow
window of cash trading. This report asks what the order book does in that window.

We use order-by-order data, which records every entry, modification and cancellation
separately, and reconstruct the limit order book second by second. Each of
\DesignExpirySessions{} expiry Thursdays in 2022 is compared against a matched control
session in the same month, across \DesignSymbols{} securities. The sample is
\SampleOrderEventsMillions{} million order events and \SampleTradesMillions{} million
trades, from which \SampleSnapshots{} book reconstructions are built.

We compute the settlement price itself, which lets us ask how far it ends up from the
opening mid and from the close, and we separate two explanations for what changes in the
window: prices being pushed in one direction, and more participants trading in both. Of
\TestSpecified{} hypotheses, \TestSupported{} are supported and \TestContradicted{} are
rejected with the effect running the other way.

\end{abstract}
