\section{The settlement mechanism}\label{sec:background}

Equity derivatives on the National Stock Exchange expire on the last Thursday of each
calendar month. For stock futures the settlement price is the volume-weighted average price
of the underlying share in the cash market between \DesignWindowStart{} and
\DesignWindowEnd{} on that day, a window of \DesignWindowMinutes{} minutes ending at the
close.

Three properties of this arrangement shape what follows.

The window is known in advance, so anyone who wants to act in it can plan rather than react.
The window is short, so a single participant's volume can be a noticeable share of the total
traded in it, particularly in a security that does not trade much. And because the
settlement is an average rather than a single print, moving it requires trading across the
window rather than placing one order at the close; if there is a footprint, it should be
visible in the shape of activity across the half hour, not only at its end.

\subsection{Order types that matter here}

The cash market runs a continuous limit order book with price-time priority. Two order-level
features are used directly in this report.

A \emph{disclosed quantity} instruction lets a participant display less than the full size of
an order. Only the displayed slice rests in the visible book; as it fills, the concealed
remainder replenishes it, and each replenishment goes to the back of the queue at that price.
The trade-off is deliberate---the participant gives up queue priority in exchange for not
revealing size---and both sides of it are recorded in the order file.

An \emph{immediate-or-cancel} instruction executes against whatever is available at
submission and cancels the remainder. It is the usual way to take liquidity without leaving
a resting order behind, and its rate of use is a reasonable proxy for how urgent the flow is.

\subsection{Why concealment is relevant}

A participant accumulating or distributing stock into a benchmark window has an obvious
reason to hide size. A displayed order of unusual size announces both the direction and the
scale of what is being attempted, which invites others to trade ahead of it. Concealment is
also entirely routine for large institutional orders on any day, so its level says little on
its own; the comparison against the control session is what carries the information.
